## What Loss of Pay means

Loss of Pay (LOP) is a deduction from your salary for a working day that is not paid. Weekends,
public holidays and approved leave are never treated as LOP.

## When a day becomes LOP

A working day is treated as LOP when, at the **payroll cutoff**, any of these is true:

- there is **no timesheet entry and no attendance record** for the day;
- you were absent without approved leave;
- you took leave beyond your remaining entitlement.

The payroll cutoff for a day is **5 working days** after that day. A timesheet logged before the
cutoff prevents LOP for that day.

## How the deduction is calculated

The deduction for one LOP day is your **annual base salary divided by 260** (the number of working
days in a year). For example, on a salary of $104,000 one LOP day is $400.00.

LOP is deducted in the **next pay run after the cutoff** and appears on your payslip as a separate
line, "Loss of Pay", with the dates it covers.

## Reversing an LOP deduction

If you worked on a day that was treated as LOP:

1. Log the missing hours in your timesheet. Late entries are accepted for up to **60 days**.
2. Raise a payroll request asking for an LOP review, with the date and the payslip it appeared on.

Payroll reviews LOP requests within **3 working days**. A confirmed reversal is refunded in the
next pay run, or in an off-cycle payment if it is over $200 / £150.

## Who this applies to

This policy applies to full-time employees. Contractors are paid by their agency for the hours the
agency approves, so company LOP does not apply to them.
